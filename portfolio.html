MakeMyTrip Context-Aware Stay Discovery Assistant

Product Management with Applied AI · From user research to a working AI prototype, PRD, evaluation and growth plan.

One-line summary: Travelers don't lack options, they lack confidence in narrowing them. I designed an assistant that turns a plain-language trip description into a small, explained, evidence-backed shortlist of stays, then hands off to MakeMyTrip.
Live prototype ↗Demo dataset (Airtable) ↗

1 · The problem

Hotel search gives travelers many listings, ranked by popularity with sponsored placements mixed in. A high rating or a low price doesn't mean a property fits this trip: the family, the budget, the purpose. People end up cross-checking Google, Instagram, YouTube, reviews and friends by hand.

2 · Research: what I learned

6 user interviews

Solo travelers, couples, families and a group of 11, plus an affinity map across 9 clusters.

Competitive teardown

MakeMyTrip/Myra, Google Hotels, Airbnb and Booking.com, plus a Nielsen heuristic review of Myra.

Frameworks

JTBD, AEIOU, journey map, SWOT, Porter's Five Forces, 2 proto-personas.

Key insights

  • Choice overload came up in every interview. The real job is narrowing and validating, not discovering.
  • Surface criteria mislead. Participants described 5-star or highly rated stays that failed on noise, hygiene, location or facilities.
  • Trust is built through evidence: recent reviews, photos and first-hand accounts. Promoted or vague content erodes it.
  • 4 of 6 already use AI for planning, but as a starting point they verify, not as an authority. Staleness and generic answers were their main worries.
Strategic insight: Conversational search is no longer novel (Myra and Booking.com already have it). So I positioned the product around a specific decision problem: context → shortlist → evidence → refinement → handoff, not "another travel chatbot."

3 · Decisions and trade-offs

I scored 10 opportunity areas on pain, evidence, value, feasibility and differentiation (out of 25), then prioritised 20 features into P0/P1/P2.

  • What I said no to: booking/payment and full itineraries, even though one interviewee wanted route planning. The evidence was thinner and the build cost far higher. The MVP stops at a MakeMyTrip handoff.
  • Stars count for 5%. Popularity shouldn't win when a property is a poor fit for the trip.
  • The AI can't invent constraints. "Affordable" or "cheaper" is a preference. Only "under ₹3,500" becomes a hard budget cap.

4 · How AI and rules work together

I used AI where the problem is language and used deterministic logic where users need predictability. The LLM never picks properties from memory.

  1. AIExtract trip context (destination, travelers, intent, budget, transport) from free text
  2. UserReview and correct the interpreted context before anything is recommended
  3. RulesApply hard constraints (destination, property type, numeric budget)
  4. RulesRank eligible stays with a 7-factor Context Fit Score
  5. RulesRetrieve linked evidence records for the shortlist
  6. AIWrite a grounded "Why this fits" explanation with caveats, using only retrieved data
  7. AIHandle refinement ("cheaper" + "closer to the beach") while keeping both

5 · Responsible AI and evaluation

  • No invented prices, amenities or reviews. Uncertainty is shown, and users can correct the extracted context.
  • 12/12 assertion checks passed (destination grounding, no invented budget, evidence grounding, hard constraints, handoff and more).
  • 43/50 (86%) on a 10-dimension rubric I defined. Strongest: constraint handling, explainability, Responsible AI. Weakest: no map, no side-by-side comparison, limited multi-turn negotiation.
Honest limits: The prototype runs on a fictional dataset (20 destinations, 80 properties, 160 evidence records) with no live inventory. Evaluation was self-run, not a user study. I make no conversion or revenue claims without production data.

6 · Go-to-market and growth

Positioning

A pre-booking decision-support layer, embedded and free in the core experience. Paid tiers later (saved preferences, shared planning).

North Star

Confident Recommendation Rate: share of sessions where a traveler opens a recommended property or moves to booking handoff.

Funnel

Entry → qualified chat → context confirmed → recommendations → property detail → handoff, with drop-off hypotheses for each step.

Experiments

Cards with vs without "Why this fits"; top 3 vs top 5; confirm context before ranking vs rank immediately.

Artifacts in this project: PRD with 8 user stories and acceptance criteria · MVP → MVP+ → future roadmap · AI vs deterministic feature classification · Pricing and packaging recommendation.
